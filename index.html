The everyday problem

Every meal is a ride on the blood sugar roller coaster.

Blood sugar isn't something only other people worry about. Everyone rides it three times a day. What's on the plate decides whether the ride is wild or mild, and processed food is full of hidden sugars and starches.

Refined carbs: oats, pizza, sushiBalanced plates: eggs, chicken, fish with veg

1 bowl = 2 cans

Sugar is only half the story.

A bowl of white rice carries more than twice the carbohydrate of a can of fizzy drink. Gram for gram, refined starches have about twice the glycaemic impact of sugar. This is the fact that makes people stop scrolling.

Minute 0

First bite

Two strips go in as you start eating, so Reducose travels down with your food.

0 to ~1 hour

The scissors

Enzymes at the top of the small intestine cut carbs into sugar. Reducose's 1-DNJ compound looks almost like glucose, so some of those enzymes grab it and stay busy for about an hour. Less sugar is released.

Hours later

Lower gut

Around a quarter of the carbs travel on undigested, a bit like fibre. In the lower small intestine they meet the body's own fullness signals.

12 to 16 hours

Colon

Gut bacteria ferment those carbs into short-chain fatty acids, a prebiotic effect. Reducose itself is absorbed and leaves in urine within 6 to 8 hours, unchanged.

Say it right

Three laws. Follow them and you can say almost anything.

Sell the moment, not the medicine.

"For your carb-heavy meals", never "for your diabetes".

Show, don't promise.

A glucose monitor line on screen beats any sentence. Say "here's my line today", not what it means medically.

Experience is personal.

"I wasn't reaching for the biscuits" is a story. "It stops cravings" is a claim.

Say freely

  • The chromium claim, word for word
  • Award-winning white mulberry leaf extract, made using only water
  • Studied in 15+ human clinical studies
  • 450 million servings sold since 2019
  • Two strips as you start eating, no water, fits in your pocket
  • Made for carb-heavy meals: bread, pasta, rice, potatoes
  • Love your food again, take back control, your sugar buddy
  • General education: the roller coaster, hidden sugars, rice vs fizzy drink

Say with care

  • Study percentages: only as ingredient research in live Q&A, never in captions or ads
  • Energy, no 3pm slump, quieter food noise: only as "what I noticed" or "people tell us"
  • Glucose monitor on camera: show it, describe the line, no medical talk
  • Fullness: "I just wasn't thinking about snacks", never "suppresses appetite"
  • Sugar and skin: teach glycation as general science, never link it to the product

Never say

  • Weight loss, fat loss, fat burning, waist size, before and afters
  • GLP-1, Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, jabs, "nature's Ozempic"
  • Diabetes, prediabetes, HbA1c, insulin resistance as a benefit
  • Treats, cures, prevents, reverses
  • "Carb blocker" or "blocks 25% of your carbs"
  • Anti-ageing, protects collagen, fewer wrinkles
  • Endorsement by a doctor, nurse, pharmacist or a named brand

Objections

What they'll ask, and what you say.

"Does it actually work?"

"Watch my monitor. That's exactly why I'm wearing it." Then: 15+ human clinical studies and 450 million servings.

"Is it safe?"

"It's a plant extract made with water. 450 million servings since 2019 with zero quality complaints, and it's been safety-tested on top of the 15 studies."

"I'm diabetic / on medication. Can I take it?"

"Please check with your GP or pharmacist first. I can't give medical advice." Say no more than that. This matters: people on some diabetes medicines need to watch for low blood sugar.

"Will it help me lose weight?"

"It's not a weight-loss product. It's for enjoying your meals with a steadier ride."

"Is it like Ozempic or the jabs?"

"No, it's nothing like a medicine. It's a mulberry leaf extract you take with food." Then move on.

"Can I just take one strip?"

"Always two. It works like a light switch: one strip isn't enough to turn it on."

"Before or after food?"

"At the first bite. Its job is to be there when your food arrives."

"Is it just for sugar?"

"It's made for starchy carbs too: bread, pasta, rice, potatoes."

"Why strips, not tablets?"

"No water, melts in seconds, and it lives in your pocket for the restaurant or the office."

"£29.99 is a lot."

"It's about £2 a meal, less than a coffee, and you only need it for your carb-heavy ones."

"What if I'm pregnant or breastfeeding?"

"Check with your doctor before using any food supplement."

"What does it taste like?"

"Guava. Sweet, light, and gone in seconds."
